# RWA Geospatial Underwriting – Analysis Outputs

## 1. Overview

This folder contains the analytical outputs generated by the **Real Estate Real-World Asset (RWA) Underwriting and Geospatial Screening Pipeline**.

The outputs combine property-level financial modelling with geospatial and environmental evidence to support preliminary screening of illustrative real-estate assets.

The analysis integrates financial underwriting, coastal-change observations, land-cover classification, terrain and elevation analysis, river-flood modelling, and historical and future coastal-flood scenarios.

The purpose is to demonstrate how geospatial information can complement financial analysis in an RWA property-screening workflow.

**Important:** This project is a research and technical proof of concept. It is not a production-ready property valuation, investment recommendation, legal due-diligence system, flood certification or operational tokenization platform.

---

## 2. Project Evidence Framework

The outputs are organised around the following analytical components:

| Component | Dataset or analytical method | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Property financial underwriting | Python and Pandas | Rental yield, valuation adjustments and preliminary token-eligibility screening |
| Coastal-change analysis | Digital Earth Africa Coastlines | Coastal movement and nearby observation evidence |
| Land-cover assessment | ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 | Built-up areas, vegetation, water and other land-cover classes |
| Elevation and terrain analysis | NASA SRTM | Elevation, terrain variation and slope |
| River-flood assessment | JRC Global River Flood Hazard Maps v2.1 | Modelled river-flood depths within surrounding buffers |
| Historical coastal-flood assessment | WRI Aqueduct Flood Hazard Maps V2 | Historical coastal inundation evidence |
| Future coastal-flood assessment | WRI Aqueduct Flood Hazard Maps V2 | Comparison of future climate and sea-level scenarios |

## 4. Summary of Key Analytical Findings

### 4.1 Land-Cover Assessment

ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 was used to examine land-cover composition within 100 m and 300 m buffers around the five illustrative properties.

The assessment identified variations in built-up areas, natural vegetation, cropland, permanent water and other land-cover classes.

### 4.2 Elevation and Terrain Assessment

SRTM elevation data were used to derive point elevation and surrounding terrain statistics, including mean, minimum and maximum elevation and estimated slope.

The results provide preliminary topographic context for the sample locations.

### 4.3 River-Flood Assessment

JRC 100-year river-flood depth observations were examined within four buffer distances.

Modelled flood-depth pixels were detected in the surrounding areas of four illustrative properties within the tested 5 km search radius. No corresponding pixels were detected around the Kumasi sample location within that radius.

The retrieved maximum depth in the buffers containing mapped observations was 1.0 m.

These are surrounding-area observations, not measurements of inundation at verified property boundaries.

### 4.4 Historical Coastal-Flood Assessment

The selected WRI historical 100-year baseline had valid raster coverage across the 15 buffer assessments.

No positive modelled coastal inundation depth was detected in the sampled pixels under this selected configuration.

### 4.5 Future Coastal-Flood Assessment

The future scenario analysis examined RCP4.5 and RCP8.5 for 2030, 2050 and 2080, with different subsidence settings and sea-level projection percentiles.

The 540 combined future observations reported valid model coverage with no positive modelled coastal inundation depth detected within the tested buffers.

This result describes the selected model outputs and assumptions; it does not demonstrate that future flood risk is absent.

## 5. Data Quality and Methodological Limitations

The following limitations must be considered when interpreting these outputs:

1. **Illustrative property locations:** The five property coordinates are synthetic or illustrative inputs and are not verified cadastral parcel coordinates.

2. **Buffer-level measurements:** Spatial measurements within 100 m, 300 m, 500 m, 1 km, 3 km and 5 km buffers describe the surrounding areas, not the precise conditions at a property's legal boundaries.

3. **Different spatial resolutions:** The source datasets operate at different spatial resolutions. In particular, the approximately 1 km WRI coastal-flood data are unsuitable for detailed parcel-level inundation assessment.

4. **Modelled flood evidence:** The JRC and WRI outputs represent modelled flood conditions under specified assumptions. Absence of mapped inundation does not demonstrate flood safety.

5. **Environmental interpretation:** Built-up land, vegetation, water presence, elevation and slope are contextual indicators. They should not automatically be interpreted as property-risk scores.

6. **Financial assumptions:** Property values, rental income, coordinates and other illustrative inputs require independent verification before real investment or underwriting decisions.

7. **No legal verification:** This project does not verify land ownership, title registration, cadastral boundaries, planning permission, encumbrances or regulatory compliance.

8. **No production tokenization:** The project demonstrates preliminary analytical and smart-contract concepts. It does not mint actual property-backed tokens or establish legal ownership of real estate.

The outputs should therefore be interpreted as preliminary research and screening evidence requiring further field verification, authoritative property information and professional technical assessment.
